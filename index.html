01 / Territory point of view

15,000 districts.
One focused plan.

I wouldn't attempt to sell to the whole market at once. I would build a research-backed account universe, select a realistic first wave, and protect time for both high-value and faster-moving opportunities.

My central thesis

Prioritize where need, access, and a credible buying path intersect.

Enrollment tells me potential deal size. It doesn't tell me which district is ready to engage. I want a balanced portfolio that creates near-term pipeline while developing larger, more complex opportunities.

What earns a place on the list?

✓
Student safety workflow need. Evidence of a relevant operational problem, validated through discovery.
✓
Access to the right people. A credible path to an operational champion and decision-maker.
✓
Purchasing reality. Budget, timing, and procurement path worth pursuing.
✓
Opportunity economics. District size and likely effort to close.
How I'd say it: “I don't want the biggest list. I want the list I can work well enough to create qualified opportunities.”

value and velocity.

Three size segments, different buying motions, and one clear allocation of time. These thresholds are starting hypotheses to validate against Bark's contract economics and actual district enrollment data.

Why these counts?

Enterprise accounts take disproportionately more preparation and committee navigation. I would work fewer of them at higher depth. Small accounts need a lighter-touch motion, allowing more accounts per hour.

The first 90 days

Learn → validate → reallocate
Days 1–30 · Build

Verify enrollment and contacts, research existing programs, start outreach, and calibrate messaging by role.

Days 31–60 · Diagnose

Compare contact access, meetings held, qualified opportunity creation, and sales-cycle obstacles by segment.

Days 61–90 · Reallocate

Move effort toward segments producing qualified pipeline per selling hour, while retaining promising long-cycle enterprise accounts.

Trade-off: “I accept a longer enterprise cycle, but I won't let it become my only path to pipeline.”

04 / Live conversation prep

Discover first.
Pitch second.

The panel will provide the district briefing on the spot. This is a conversation framework, not a script to memorize. Don't enter the room assuming the district needs a product.

The 6-move discovery flow

1

Open with permission

“I'd love to understand what you're focused on, how your team handles student safety concerns today, and then decide whether there's a reason to explore further.”

2

Understand the current process

“Walk me through what happens from the first signal of concern to the right adult taking action.”

3

Find friction and impact

“Where does that process get difficult? How often does that happen, and what does it require from your team?”

4

Define success

“If you could improve one part of that process this year, what would meaningful progress look like?”

5

Understand the decision path

“Who else owns this process, and how would the district evaluate and fund a change?”

6

Reflect, connect, and advance

Summarize the district's stated problem, connect only verified relevant Bark capabilities, and request a technical and operational follow-up if warranted.
